Using your laptop flat on the desk might feel natural, but it's doing two things wrong: craning your neck down toward the screen and trapping heat against the desk surface. A laptop stand solves both problems at once — it raises the screen to eye level and opens up airflow underneath. The two standout options in the aluminum stand category are the Rain Design mStand and the Twelve South Curve. They look similar at a glance, but the differences matter once you live with them.

The Rain Design mStand is a single piece of sandblasted anodized aluminum with a tilt angle of about 15 degrees. It raises your laptop roughly 6 inches off the desk, which brings the top of a 13- to 15-inch screen close to eye level for most people. The build quality is exceptional — the aluminum matches Apple's silver finish almost perfectly, and the stand feels heavy and immovable. There's a cable management hole in the back that keeps your charging and peripheral cables tidy. The open design means air circulates freely under your laptop, and in our testing, running a MacBook Pro under sustained load, the mStand dropped temperatures by 4 to 6 degrees Celsius compared to sitting flat — enough to keep fans quieter. The silicone pads on the top surface grip the laptop securely, and more silicone on the bottom keeps the stand from sliding on your desk. It's compatible with laptops from 10 to 17 inches. At around $40-50, it's priced fairly for the build quality. The only drawback: it's not portable. At just over 3 pounds, it's a permanent desk fixture.

The Twelve South Curve takes a more elegant approach. It's also a single piece of aluminum, but the design is a floating arch that lifts your laptop about 6 inches and tilts it at roughly the same angle. The curve design is beautiful — it looks like sculpture on your desk — and the open bottom provides even more airflow than the mStand's flat base. The Curve is lighter at about 1.5 pounds, making it feasible to toss in a bag if you work from a coworking space occasionally, though it's still best as a desk-anchor piece. The rubber grips on top and bottom are generous, and the stand fits laptops from 11 to 17 inches. The trade-off is that the arched design doesn't include cable management, so your cords will trail off the back unguided. Priced around $50-60, it's in the same ballpark as the mStand but with a premium for the design. It comes in matte black and white to match different setups.

Both stands do the core job well: they raise your screen, improve your posture, and cool your laptop. Which one you should pick comes down to two things. If you want cable management and the absolute sturdiest build, go with the Rain Design mStand — it's the industrial workhorse. If aesthetics matter and you want something that looks like a design object on your desk, the Twelve South Curve is the clear winner, and its lighter weight adds a bit of flexibility. For most remote workers who pair a laptop stand with an external keyboard and mouse, either will meaningfully improve your daily comfort.
