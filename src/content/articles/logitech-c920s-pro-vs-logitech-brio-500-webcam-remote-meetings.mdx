Your laptop's built-in camera was designed by engineers who never had to sit through a client pitch on Zoom. It's grainy, it's aimed up your nose, and it makes you look like you're dialing in from a witness protection program. If you spend more than a few hours a week on video calls, a dedicated webcam is the single biggest upgrade you can make to your remote work setup. The two most practical options right now are the Logitech C920s Pro and the Logitech Brio 500 — one is the tried-and-true workhorse, the other is the newer upstart with smarter software.

The Logitech C920s Pro has been the default recommendation for remote workers for years, and for good reason. It shoots 1080p at 30 frames per second with a 78-degree field of view — wide enough to show your face and some background, but not so wide that your messy kitchen becomes the star of the meeting. Image quality in good lighting is sharp and color-accurate; in low light, it holds up better than most laptops but does get noticeably grainy. The C920s includes a physical privacy shutter that slides over the lens, which is a small touch that eliminates the post-it-note-over-the-camera look. Dual omnidirectional microphones pick up voice clearly from about 3 feet away. It connects via USB-A and clips securely onto any monitor or laptop screen. At around $60-70, it's been the value king for half a decade.

The Logitech Brio 500 is the newer contender, launched as part of Logitech's refreshed webcam lineup. It also shoots 1080p at 30fps but packs a wider 90-degree field of view and a significantly better sensor. Where the Brio 500 really pulls ahead is in lighting adaptation — it has Logitech's RightLight 4 technology that automatically adjusts exposure, contrast, and white balance frame by frame. The result is that you look good even with a window behind you or in a dim room, scenarios where the C920s struggles. It also has auto-framing that keeps you centered in the shot if you shift around, and a Show Mode that lets you tilt the camera down to show something on your desk. The privacy shutter is built-in and magnetic. The Brio 500 uses USB-C, which is great if your setup is modern but may need an adapter for older machines. It costs about $130.

For pure call quality in controlled lighting — say, a home office with consistent front-facing light — the C920s Pro is still excellent and leaves $60 in your pocket. The image is crisp, the mic is clear, and the privacy shutter is satisfyingly tactile. If you're in a darker room, sit near a bright window, or move around while presenting, the Brio 500's auto-adjustment and auto-framing genuinely make a visible difference to how professional you look on screen.

One thing neither webcam can fix: your audio environment. If your room echoes, invest in some basic acoustic treatment (even a rug and curtains help) and consider a dedicated USB microphone. A crisp image with hollow audio still makes for a rough meeting.

Our pick: the Logitech C920s Pro for most remote workers. It's reliable, affordable, and does the job without fuss. Spend the extra on the Brio 500 if your lighting is unpredictable, you present frequently, or you just want to look your best without thinking about camera settings.
