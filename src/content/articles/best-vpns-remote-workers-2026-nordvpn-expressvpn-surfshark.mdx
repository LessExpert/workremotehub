There is no "just quickly check something on the coffee shop Wi-Fi." The moment you connect to a public network without protection, you are broadcasting your browsing to anyone running a packet sniffer at the next table. For remote workers, this isn't theoretical — you are working from cafes, co-working spaces, airports, hotels, and Airbnbs where the network is not yours and you have no idea who else is on it. A VPN is the minimum viable security for remote work.

But VPNs are not all equal. Some slow your connection to the point where video calls drop. Some log your activity despite claiming otherwise. Some work great on your laptop but fail on your phone. We tested NordVPN, ExpressVPN, and Surfshark — the three most popular VPNs among remote workers — over two weeks on coffee shop, co-working, and home networks, measuring speed loss, connection reliability, and feature usefulness in real remote work scenarios.

NordVPN is the heavy hitter in this space with over 6,300 servers across 111 countries. What matters for remote workers: it consistently delivered the lowest speed loss in our testing. On a 150 Mbps home connection, NordVPN dropped us to about 127 Mbps on nearby servers — that's a 15 percent hit, which is excellent for a VPN. The proprietary NordLynx protocol (built on WireGuard) is the reason; it's significantly faster than OpenVPN and noticeably snappier than ExpressVPN's Lightway in side-by-side tests. NordVPN's Threat Protection feature is specifically useful for remote workers — it blocks malicious websites, trackers, and ads at the network level before they even reach your browser. It also scans downloads for malware. Meshnet, another feature, lets you create an encrypted private network between your devices, so you can access files on your home computer from anywhere as if you're on the same local network — useful if you left something on your desktop and you're working from a co-working space across town. NordVPN allows 10 simultaneous device connections. Pricing runs $12.99 month-to-month, $4.99/month on a one-year plan, and $3.39/month on the two-year plan, which is the best value among the three. The two-year plan includes three extra months free. NordVPN is based in Panama and has undergone four independent no-logs audits — the most of any VPN provider — so the privacy claims have receipts. The main downside: the Windows app interface feels a bit cluttered with features you may not use, like dark web monitoring and a data breach scanner.

ExpressVPN is the premium option — $12.95 month-to-month, $6.67/month on an annual plan, with a six-month plan at $9.99/month. It has servers in 105 countries, slightly fewer than NordVPN but with better coverage in Africa and the Middle East, which matters if you're a digital nomad moving through less-connected regions. The standout feature for remote workers is ExpressVPN's Lightway protocol, which is built from scratch for speed and connection resilience. In our testing, switching between Wi-Fi networks (co-working to coffee shop to home) was seamless — Lightway reconnected in under a second, often before we even noticed the network had changed. On a mobile hotspot from a phone with spotty LTE, ExpressVPN held a stable connection while NordVPN and Surfshark both dropped at least once during a one-hour work session. Speed loss was around 18 percent on nearby servers — very good, just slightly behind NordVPN. ExpressVPN allows 8 simultaneous devices. The apps are the most polished of the three — clean, simple, one-tap connect on every platform. ExpressVPN is based in the British Virgin Islands and has completed over a dozen independent security audits. The TrustedServer technology, which runs all servers on RAM rather than hard drives, means no data can physically persist on a server after a reboot. The main downside is price — it's the most expensive VPN here, and the annual plan doesn't include bonus months the way NordVPN does. You're paying a premium for reliability, and for remote workers who can't afford a dropped connection during a client call, that premium may be worth it.

Surfshark is the budget option that doesn't feel budget. At $15.45 month-to-month, $3.99/month on the annual plan, or $2.19/month on the two-year plan, it's the cheapest of the three by a wide margin. Surfshark uses WireGuard, like NordVPN, and the speed performance is comparable — around 17 percent speed loss on nearby servers, nearly identical to ExpressVPN. The feature that sets Surfshark apart for remote workers is unlimited simultaneous device connections. You can install it on your laptop, phone, tablet, partner's devices, and your family's computers — all under one subscription. This is a genuine value if you're managing security across a household of remote workers and devices. CleanWeb, Surfshark's ad and tracker blocker, worked well in testing and blocked most pop-ups and cookie banners on news sites, which makes research-heavy work sessions noticeably cleaner. Surfshark is based in the Netherlands and has completed one independent no-logs audit (2023), which is good but less extensive than NordVPN's four audits or ExpressVPN's dozens. The main downside: server coverage is 100 countries, and we found that some servers in less popular locations (South America, parts of Southeast Asia) showed higher latency than NordVPN's equivalent locations. The apps are well-designed but occasionally prompt you to try additional paid features like Surfshark Alert and Incogni, which gets annoying.

After two weeks of real remote work usage — video calls, large file transfers, streaming research videos, and general browsing — here is what we recommend. Pick NordVPN if you want the best overall performance and the strongest privacy audit record at the best long-term price. The NordLynx speed advantage is real, and Threat Protection is genuinely useful, not a checkbox feature. Pick ExpressVPN if you work from unreliable networks frequently — Lightway's connection resilience is best-in-class, and if you're a digital nomad bouncing between SIM cards and sketchy hotel Wi-Fi, the premium price buys you fewer dropped connections. Pick Surfshark if you need to cover many devices on a budget — unlimited connections at $2.19/month is untouchable value, and the core VPN performance is strong enough that most remote workers won't notice the difference from the others.

One final note: none of these VPNs will protect you if you log into your Google or Facebook account while connected. The VPN hides your IP address, but the moment you sign into a service that knows who you are, your activity is tied to your identity. For actual privacy, pair a VPN with a privacy-focused browser like Brave or Firefox with tracking protection enabled. And always turn on the kill switch — every VPN here has one, and it prevents data leaks if the VPN connection drops. No kill switch means one dropped connection exposes everything you were doing on that coffee shop Wi-Fi.
